# Software Architect Assignment Evaluation Rubric

## Overview
This rubric provides a structured approach to evaluating candidates for Senior/Staff Software Architect positions across both healthcare and enterprise assignments. Each criterion is scored on a 1-5 scale with specific indicators for each level.

## Scoring Scale
- **5 - Exceptional:** Exceeds expectations, demonstrates mastery
- **4 - Proficient:** Meets expectations with strong competency
- **3 - Adequate:** Meets basic expectations
- **2 - Developing:** Below expectations, needs improvement
- **1 - Inadequate:** Significantly below expectations

---

## 1. Technical Depth (40% Weight)

### Domain Understanding
**Healthcare Assignment:**
- **5:** Deep understanding of healthcare workflows, regulatory requirements, and clinical data complexities
- **4:** Good grasp of healthcare domain with appropriate consideration of HIPAA/compliance
- **3:** Basic understanding of healthcare requirements with some gaps
- **2:** Limited healthcare domain knowledge, missing key considerations
- **1:** Poor understanding of healthcare-specific challenges

**Enterprise Assignment:**
- **5:** Comprehensive understanding of enterprise software patterns, multi-tenancy, and B2B SaaS challenges
- **4:** Good grasp of enterprise architecture with solid multi-tenant design
- **3:** Basic understanding of enterprise requirements with some gaps
- **2:** Limited enterprise software knowledge, missing key patterns
- **1:** Poor understanding of enterprise-scale challenges

### Architectural Patterns & Design Principles
- **5:** Demonstrates mastery of multiple architectural patterns, applies them appropriately with clear rationale
- **4:** Shows good knowledge of relevant patterns, applies them correctly in most cases
- **3:** Uses basic architectural patterns appropriately
- **2:** Limited pattern knowledge, some inappropriate applications
- **1:** Poor understanding of architectural patterns

### Technology Choices & Justification
- **5:** Excellent technology selections with detailed trade-off analysis and alternatives considered
- **4:** Good technology choices with solid justification
- **3:** Reasonable technology selections with basic rationale
- **2:** Some questionable technology choices, weak justification
- **1:** Poor technology selections without proper justification

### Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios
- **5:** Comprehensive consideration of failure modes, edge cases, and recovery strategies
- **4:** Good coverage of major failure scenarios with mitigation plans
- **3:** Basic consideration of common failure cases
- **2:** Limited failure scenario planning
- **1:** Little to no consideration of failure modes

---

## 2. Trade-off Analysis (30% Weight)

### Decision Rationale
- **5:** Clear, well-reasoned explanations for all major architectural decisions with alternatives considered
- **4:** Good rationale for most decisions with some alternatives discussed
- **3:** Basic explanations for key decisions
- **2:** Limited rationale, few alternatives considered
- **1:** Poor or missing explanations for architectural choices

### Business vs. Technical Constraints
- **5:** Excellent balance of business needs and technical constraints with clear prioritization
- **4:** Good understanding of business-technical trade-offs
- **3:** Basic consideration of business and technical factors
- **2:** Limited integration of business and technical concerns
- **1:** Poor understanding of business-technical balance

### Risk Assessment
- **5:** Comprehensive risk analysis with mitigation strategies and contingency planning
- **4:** Good risk identification with appropriate mitigation approaches
- **3:** Basic risk assessment with some mitigation ideas
- **2:** Limited risk consideration
- **1:** Little to no risk analysis

### Cost vs. Benefit Analysis
- **5:** Detailed cost-benefit analysis considering development, operational, and maintenance costs
- **4:** Good cost-benefit considerations for major decisions
- **3:** Basic cost awareness in decision making
- **2:** Limited cost consideration
- **1:** Poor understanding of cost implications

---

## 3. Security & Compliance (20% Weight)

### Security Architecture Design
**Healthcare Assignment:**
- **5:** Comprehensive HIPAA-compliant security design with defense-in-depth approach
- **4:** Good security architecture with appropriate healthcare compliance measures
- **3:** Basic security measures with some compliance considerations
- **2:** Limited security design, missing key healthcare requirements
- **1:** Inadequate security architecture for healthcare data

**Enterprise Assignment:**
- **5:** Robust multi-tenant security model with comprehensive enterprise integration security
- **4:** Good multi-tenant security with solid enterprise security practices
- **3:** Basic multi-tenant security with some enterprise considerations
- **2:** Limited security design for multi-tenant environment
- **1:** Inadequate security architecture for enterprise multi-tenancy

### Regulatory Compliance Understanding
- **5:** Deep understanding of relevant regulations with practical implementation approaches
- **4:** Good regulatory knowledge with appropriate compliance measures
- **3:** Basic compliance understanding with standard approaches
- **2:** Limited regulatory knowledge
- **1:** Poor understanding of compliance requirements

### Privacy & Data Protection
- **5:** Privacy-by-design principles with comprehensive data protection strategies
- **4:** Good privacy considerations with solid data protection measures
- **3:** Basic privacy and data protection approaches
- **2:** Limited privacy considerations
- **1:** Inadequate privacy and data protection design

### Audit & Monitoring
- **5:** Comprehensive audit logging and monitoring strategy with automated compliance reporting
- **4:** Good audit and monitoring design with compliance considerations
- **3:** Basic audit logging and monitoring approaches
- **2:** Limited audit and monitoring capabilities
- **1:** Inadequate audit and monitoring design

---

## 4. Communication & Presentation (10% Weight)

### Clarity & Organization
- **5:** Exceptionally clear and well-organized presentation with logical flow
- **4:** Clear presentation with good organization and structure
- **3:** Generally clear with adequate organization
- **2:** Some unclear sections, organization could be improved
- **1:** Unclear presentation with poor organization

### Technical Communication
- **5:** Excellent ability to explain complex concepts to diverse audiences with appropriate level of detail
- **4:** Good technical communication skills with audience awareness
- **3:** Adequate technical communication abilities
- **2:** Some difficulty explaining technical concepts clearly
- **1:** Poor technical communication skills

### Visual Aids & Diagrams
- **5:** Excellent use of diagrams and visual aids that enhance understanding
- **4:** Good visual aids that support the presentation effectively
- **3:** Adequate use of visual aids
- **2:** Limited or ineffective use of visual aids
- **1:** Poor or confusing visual aids

### Q&A Responsiveness
- **5:** Excellent responsiveness to questions with thoughtful, detailed answers
- **4:** Good handling of questions with solid responses
- **3:** Adequate response to most questions
- **2:** Some difficulty answering questions effectively
- **1:** Poor responsiveness or inability to answer key questions

---

## Overall Scoring

### Score Calculation
- Technical Depth: ___/5 × 40% = ___/2.0
- Trade-off Analysis: ___/5 × 30% = ___/1.5
- Security & Compliance: ___/5 × 20% = ___/1.0
- Communication: ___/5 × 10% = ___/0.5
- **Total Score: ___/5.0**

### Recommendation Thresholds
- **4.0-5.0:** Strong Hire - Exceeds role requirements
- **3.5-3.9:** Hire - Meets role requirements with good potential
- **3.0-3.4:** Borderline - Meets minimum requirements, consider team needs
- **2.5-2.9:** No Hire - Below role requirements
- **Below 2.5:** Strong No Hire - Significantly below requirements

### Additional Evaluation Notes

#### Red Flags (Automatic No Hire Regardless of Score)
- Fundamental security vulnerabilities in design
- Complete lack of compliance consideration for regulated industries
- Inability to articulate basic architectural concepts
- Plagiarism or use of inappropriate external resources during assignment

#### Bonus Considerations (Can elevate borderline candidates)
- Innovative solutions to complex problems
- Exceptional consideration of operational aspects
- Strong focus on maintainability and technical debt prevention
- Evidence of learning from past architectural decisions

#### Assignment-Specific Considerations

**Healthcare Assignment:**
- Extra weight on compliance and security given regulatory environment
- Consider candidate's ability to handle sensitive data scenarios
- Evaluate understanding of clinical workflow impacts

**Enterprise Assignment:**
- Extra weight on scalability and multi-tenancy design
- Consider operational complexity and cost optimization
- Evaluate understanding of enterprise integration challenges

---

## Interviewer Guidelines

### Before the Presentation
1. Review the candidate's submission thoroughly
2. Prepare specific questions about their design choices
3. Identify areas where you want deeper explanation
4. Have backup questions ready for different skill levels

### During the Presentation
1. Allow candidate to present without interruption initially
2. Take detailed notes on each evaluation criterion
3. Ask clarifying questions during designated Q&A time
4. Probe deeper on critical architectural decisions

### After the Presentation
1. Complete scoring immediately while details are fresh
2. Write detailed feedback for each major criterion
3. Provide specific examples from the presentation
4. Include recommendations for improvement areas

### Calibration Notes
- Compare scores across multiple interviewers if available
- Consider the candidate's experience level relative to role requirements
- Account for presentation nervousness vs. actual technical capability
- Focus on architectural thinking over presentation polish
